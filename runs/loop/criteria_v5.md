# Holistic Scoring Guide (1–6): Explanatory essay on the advantages of limiting car usage

Give ONE holistic score from 1 to 6. Judge mainly **framing (thesis/introduction/conclusion), organization around advantages, breadth and density of accurate source detail with explicit citation, and whether benefits are stated and explained**. Spelling and grammar matter little. Use the whole scale and do NOT default to 3. In a typical set about 7% are 1, 21% are 2, 35% are 3, 27% are 4, 9–10% are 5, and a 6 is very rare. If more than about 40% of your scores are 3, re-check: the weakest 3s are probably 2s, and the strongest 3s are 4s. Look at your 4s too: the ones built around advantages with cited multi-source evidence should be 5s. If you give almost no 5s, you are too strict at the top.

## Step-by-step procedure
1. **Copying/fact-list check.** Is the body mostly copied, near-copied, garbled, or a list of source facts with no thesis, no introduction or conclusion, and no own comment at all (or only throwaway lines such as "maybe this is good")? If yes, score 1.
2. **Structure.** Is there a thesis or stated purpose about the advantages of limiting cars, ideally previewing reasons? Are body paragraphs built around **advantages** (pollution/health, stress, traffic, cost, safety, community life, changing culture) or only around **sources**? Is there a conclusion?
3. **Development.** Does the writer connect evidence (quote, statistic, example) to why it is an advantage? One-line reactions at the end of a paragraph do NOT count as development. Repeated "evidence + comment" under advantage topic sentences does, even if wordy or error-filled. Paragraphs that drift into reporting trends or facts without linking them to a benefit are weak development.
4. **Sources.** Count sources used with specific details (of four) and details per source. Three or four sources cited explicitly (by number, author, or place) with specifics, gathered under advantage topic sentences, is the strongest pattern. Thin use (one or two details per source) is weak even if all four are named. Minor factual slips and misreadings cost little.
5. **Language.** Errors lower the score only when meaning is hard to follow (at most one point).

## Score levels

**6 (very rare):** A strong 5 plus sustained "evidence + own commentary" in every body paragraph, an acknowledged counterpoint or objection answered with reasons, and a clear sense of audience or genuine synthesis. Long, fully developed advantage essays with several quotes explained and objections addressed can reach 6 even with many errors.

**5 (strong):** Introduction with a thesis (usually previewing advantages), a conclusion (a restatement is fine), and body paragraphs organized by advantage, each stating the benefit and supporting it with cited details from several sources (three or four overall). Explanation may be brief or repetitive, and misreadings or errors are acceptable. Do NOT lower such an essay to 4 because explanation is "uneven," the conclusion only restates, one source is unused, or a detail is misread. A source-by-source essay can also be a 5 only if each source paragraph is substantial (many specifics, benefits explicitly explained, not just one-line reactions) and the introduction and conclusion frame the benefits.

**4 (competent):** Clear thesis, introduction, body paragraphs built around distinct reasons or advantages, a conclusion, and the writer explaining reasons in their own words. Source support may be thinner (one or two sources or few details), but the logic is easy to follow. Also a 4: an opinion-and-reasons essay with no sources but with intro, several distinct advantages each developed in its own paragraph, and a conclusion. Uneven explanation, errors, or few sources do not push such an essay down to 3. Source-by-source essays with moderate detail and benefits explained in each paragraph can be 4.

**3 (developing):** A recognizable position with an attempt at structure (intro, ordered reasons, conclusion, or topic-based paragraphs), and some relevant support, but explanation of why details are advantages is only occasional. Includes: mostly paraphrased source facts with a few real comments; generic or repetitive reasoning; loose organization; a weak or missing introduction or conclusion; advantage-organized paragraphs that mostly report trends without linking them to benefits. Also a 3 (even 4 if reasoning is clear): a messy essay mixing copied source fragments with the writer's own reasons (cost, safety, stress, pollution) under an intro, ordered body, and conclusion. Short source-by-source paragraphs with one or two details per source and brief reactions are 2, or 3 only if most paragraphs clearly state why the details are advantages.

**2 (limited):** Mainly reports what sources say in the writer's own words, usually one paragraph per source, with only short closing remarks or brief reactions. Thesis is vague; the conclusion is generic or absent. This applies even with an intro and conclusion, three or four sources, quotes with citations, and clean writing, if details per source are few and commentary is one line. Also a 2: a brief or rambling opinion with a few ideas and little support; source retelling with the writer's own (even mistaken or off-base) opinions and comments; source fragments plus a slight own point.

**1 (minimal):** No real argument or explanation.
- Copied or near-copied source sentences, or disconnected facts listed one after another, with no thesis/intro/conclusion and no explanation (even if all four sources are touched).
- Source-by-source retelling with only "maybe it's good"-type remarks, especially if facts are garbled or mixed up, no opening or closing.
- Personal feelings or off-topic musings that never list or explain advantages, or incoherent, nearly unreadable writing.
- If torn between 1 and 2, choose 1 only when the text is copied, a pure fact list, or has no comment of its own. If the writer paraphrases in own words and offers own opinions or reasons, choose 2.

## Decision rules
- Mostly copied or lightly reworded source sentences, or a fact list, with no thesis and no explanation: 1. A multi-paragraph shape does not rescue it. Never 3.
- Source-by-source retelling with few details per source and one-line comments: 2 (1 if copied or lacking thesis/intro/conclusion and own comment). Never 4 or 5, even with intro, conclusion, and three sources.
- Thesis + intro + conclusion + paragraphs organized by advantage with explained evidence: at least 4.
- Meets 4 and also cites three or more sources with specifics inside advantage paragraphs, benefits stated in most body paragraphs: 5.
- Using all four sources does not by itself lift an essay above 2; details must be tied to advantages.
- No source evidence: at most 4, and only if well organized by distinct advantages with real explanation. Unorganized or opinion-only essays are 1–2 (3 at most).
- Surface errors alone never move an essay more than one point. Do not penalize quotes with citations; judge the commentary around them and the density of detail.

## Calibration
- **1 vs 2:** Copied or pasted content, fact list, rambling, or only "maybe" remarks = 1. Own-wording report with some framing and a few genuine own comments = 2.
- **2 vs 3:** Each paragraph tells what one source says with a one-line reaction = 2. Paragraphs repeatedly say why details are advantages, or the writer gives own ordered reasons with intro and conclusion = 3.
- **3 vs 4:** Can you list the main reasons, each with its own paragraph, own-word explanation, intro, and conclusion? Yes = 4. Mostly restated facts, trend reporting, occasional comments, or loose structure = 3.
- **4 vs 5:** Thesis previewing advantages, advantage topic paragraphs, cited evidence from 3+ sources, benefits stated in most body paragraphs, a conclusion? Yes = 5. Only one or two sources, source-by-source with thin paragraphs, or noticeably list-like = 4 or lower.
- **5 vs 6:** Only with sustained quote-and-commentary throughout plus a counterpoint answered, synthesis, or genuine insight.
