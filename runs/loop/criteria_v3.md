# Holistic Scoring Guide (1–6): Explanatory essay on the advantages of limiting car usage

Give ONE holistic score from 1 to 6. Judge mainly **ideas, organization around advantages, accurate use of several sources, and how well evidence is explained**. Spelling and grammar matter little. Use the whole scale and do NOT default to 3. In a typical set about 7% are 1, 21% are 2, 35% are 3, 27% are 4, 9–10% are 5, and a 6 is very rare. If more than about 40% of your scores are 3, re-check: the weakest 3s are probably 2s (or 1s), and the strongest 3s are 4s. Also look at your 4s: the most thorough ones (several sources with specific details, explained, with intro and conclusion) should be 5s. If you give almost no 5s, you are too strict at the top.

## Step-by-step procedure
1. **Copying/fact-list check.** Is the body mostly copied, near-copied, garbled, or a bare list of facts with no thesis, introduction, conclusion, or explanation? If yes, score 1 (see rules below).
2. **Structure.** Is there a thesis or stated purpose about the advantages of limiting cars, ideally previewing reasons? Are body paragraphs built around advantages (pollution/health, stress, traffic, cost, safety, community life, changing culture), or only around sources? Is there a conclusion?
3. **Development.** In body paragraphs: claim, then evidence (quote, statistic, example), then the writer's own comment on why it is an advantage? Brief tag-on remarks such as "maybe this is a good thing" do NOT count as explanation. Repeated quote-plus-comment under advantage topic sentences counts as real development, even if wordy or repetitive.
4. **Sources.** Count sources used with specific, accurate details (out of four). Three or four, tied to claims, is a strong sign. Minor factual slips cost little; serious misreadings (garbling what the source says) cost more.
5. **Language.** Errors lower the score only when meaning is hard to follow (at most one point). Many errors alone never pull down an organized, developed essay.

## Score levels

**6 (very rare):** A strong 5 plus a clear sense of audience, an acknowledged counterpoint answered with reasons, or genuine synthesis across sources, sustained throughout.

**5 (strong):** Clear thesis (often previewing the advantages) in an introduction, a real conclusion, and body paragraphs that explain why things are advantages. Draws specific, mostly accurate details from three or four sources (citing them by name or number is fine) and comments on them. Paragraphs may be organized by advantage OR by source/place, as long as the writer repeatedly explains benefits (health, less pollution, less stress, community improvement, savings) rather than just retelling. Detail is rich and reasoning is sustained across the essay. Misreadings, repetition, and many surface errors are allowed. An essay with a previewed thesis, 3+ sources used with specifics, advantage commentary in most body paragraphs, and a conclusion is a 5, not a 4.

**4 (competent):** Clear thesis, introduction, body paragraphs built around distinct reasons or advantages, a conclusion, and the writer explaining the reasons in their own words. Source support may be thin (one or two sources), uneven, or list-like, but the logic is easy to follow. Also a 4: an opinion-and-reasons essay with intro, reasons, and conclusion that explains its points, even with weak source use or frequent errors.

**3 (developing):** A recognizable position and some relevant support, with paragraphs that have topics, but explanation of why details are advantages is only occasional. Mostly paraphrased source facts with some real comments, generic or repetitive reasoning, loose organization, or a weak or missing introduction or conclusion. Also fits opinion essays with partly illogical support. Clearly more than retelling, but lacks consistent advantage-by-advantage explanation.

**2 (limited):** Mainly reports what sources say, usually one paragraph per source or per article ("Rosenthal says… Duffer says… Selsky says…"), with at most short closing remarks or brief reactions about advantages. The thesis is vague, and the conclusion is generic or absent. This applies even with an intro and conclusion, three or four sources, quotes with citations, and fairly clean writing. Also a 2: a brief or rambling opinion with a few ideas and little evidence, or source fragments plus a slight own point.

**1 (minimal):** No real argument or explanation.
- Copied or near-copied source sentences, or disconnected facts listed one after another, with no thesis, no introduction or conclusion, and no explanation of advantages (even if all four sources are touched).
- Source-by-source retelling in which facts are garbled, misunderstood, or mixed up (e.g., wrong claims about what a city did or what people do), with only a throwaway "maybe it's good" line.
- Incoherent, rambling, vague, or off-topic writing that never uses concrete source detail, or is nearly unreadable.

## Decision rules
- Mostly copied or lightly reworded source sentences, or a fact list, with no thesis and no explanation: 1. A multi-paragraph shape does not rescue it. Never 3.
- Source-by-source retelling with no advantage-focused topic sentences: 2 (or 1 if garbled, no framing, or no thesis/intro/conclusion). Retelling in which most paragraphs also clearly say why the details are advantages: 3 or higher.
- Thesis + intro + conclusion + paragraphs organized by advantage with explained evidence: at least 4.
- Meets 4 and also uses three or more sources with specific details and commentary across most body paragraphs: 5.
- Using all four sources does not by itself lift an essay above 2; details must be tied to advantages. Using few sources does not cap a well-reasoned essay at 3.
- No source evidence, only unsupported opinion: at most 3 (usually 2).
- Off-topic claims, unsupported statistics, or loose analogies lower the score by about one level but do not cap an otherwise well-developed essay below 3.
- Surface errors alone never move an essay by more than one point. Do not penalize quotes with citations; judge the commentary around them.

## Calibration
- **1 vs 2:** No framing (no thesis, intro, or conclusion), fact list or pasted or garbled content, or incoherent rambling = 1. Readable, own-wording report with some framing (an intro or conclusion, or a few relevant comments) = 2.
- **2 vs 3:** Each paragraph mainly tells what one source says, with only a closing remark = 2. Paragraphs repeatedly say why the details are advantages, with a recognizable overall position = 3.
- **3 vs 4:** Can you list the main reasons, each with its own paragraph, explanation, intro, and conclusion? Yes = 4. Mostly restated facts, occasional comments, or loose structure = 3.
- **4 vs 5:** Thesis previewing advantages, 3+ sources with accurate specifics, explanation in most body paragraphs, and a real conclusion? Yes = 5. Clear but thin, one- or two-source, or noticeably list-like = 4.
- **5 vs 6:** Only with a counterpoint, synthesis, or genuine insight sustained throughout.
