# Holistic Scoring Guide (1–6): Explanatory essay on the advantages of limiting car usage

Give ONE holistic score from 1 to 6. Judge mainly **ideas, development of evidence, and organization**. Language errors matter only a little (see Step 4). Use the whole scale: do NOT default to 3. In a typical set of essays about 7% are 1, 21% are 2, 36% are 3, 27% are 4, 8% are 5, and rarely a 6. If you find yourself giving most essays a 3, re-check each one against the 2/4 descriptions below.

## Step-by-step procedure
1. **Structure.** Does the essay have a thesis or stated purpose (the advantages of limiting cars)? Does it have an introduction, body paragraphs organized around distinct advantages or ideas (e.g., pollution, stress, traffic, health, cost, safety, community life), and a conclusion?
2. **Development.** For each body paragraph, is there a claim, then evidence, then an explanation of how the evidence shows an advantage? Or are facts only listed or pasted?
3. **Sources.** How many of the four sources are used? Are the details specific and accurate, and are they explained in the writer's own words? Is the essay overly dependent on one source? Minor factual slips (a wrong number or a misattributed detail) lower the score only slightly.
4. **Language.** Spelling, grammar, and word choice are a minor factor. Do NOT lower an essay by more than one point for errors alone. Lower it only if errors make the meaning hard to follow. An organized, well-developed essay with many spelling or grammar errors can still earn a 4, 5, or even 6.

## Score levels

**6 (rare, exceptional):** Fully developed throughout. Clear thesis and a strong introduction and conclusion. Every body paragraph has a claim, specific evidence from several sources, and real explanation. The essay goes beyond summary: it addresses a counterpoint or objection, draws connections between sources, or shows genuine insight. Smooth, purposeful progression. Minor errors are acceptable. Give a 6 only if the essay is clearly better than a good 5.

**5 (strong):** Clear thesis and a well-organized multi-paragraph structure. Uses evidence from at least three sources with specific details that are integrated and explained (not just quoted). Body paragraphs are substantially developed, with reasoning that connects the evidence to the advantages. Coherent flow with a real conclusion. May contain some factual slips, repetitive phrasing, or many surface errors. Do not drop a well-developed, well-sourced essay to 3 or 4 because of spelling and grammar.

**4 (competent):** Clear thesis and clear organization: the essay has an introduction, body paragraphs built around distinct reasons or advantages, and a conclusion. The writer explains the reasons in their own words and supports at least some of them with source evidence. Source use may be limited (one or two sources, or only brief references), and development may be uneven, but the logic is easy to follow. An essay with a clear reasons-based structure and mostly sensible explanation is a 4, even if source use is thin or errors are frequent. A 4 also fits an essay that uses several sources accurately with some explanation but is somewhat repetitive or list-like.

**3 (developing):** A recognizable position and some relevant support, but with clear weaknesses: loose or generic organization, evidence that is mostly summarized or quoted with little explanation, reasoning that is repetitive, vague, or partly illogical (e.g., irrelevant analogies, unsupported broad claims), or an introduction or conclusion that is missing or merely restated. Typical 3s are source-driven reports with a position but little analysis, or opinion essays with weak or confused support. Score 3 when the essay is clearly better than a list of facts but lacks the clear structure and explanation of a 4.

**2 (limited):** The essay mostly lists or summarizes facts from the sources, often source by source, with little or no explanation of how they show advantages. It may contain heavily copied or lightly reworded source sentences, a vague or missing thesis, little transition between ideas, and an absent or token conclusion. Alternatively it is a brief or off-track opinion with almost no evidence. Some explanation or a partial attempt at a point is present.

**1 (minimal):** No real argument or explanation. Typical patterns:
- Mostly strings of copied or near-copied source sentences and fragments, with no organization of its own, no introduction or conclusion, and no explanation of advantages.
- Source-by-source retelling with no stated point, or with garbled or misunderstood content.
- Off-topic or nearly unreadable writing.

## Decision rules
- If the body is mostly copied or near-copied source sentences with no explanation or own organization, the score is at most 2. If it also has no introduction or conclusion and reads as pasted fragments, give 1.
- A pure source-by-source summary with no stated advantages or reasoning is 1 or 2 (2 only if there is some explanation or a real attempt at a conclusion).
- An essay with a thesis, reason-based paragraphs, and at least some evidence or sensible explanation is at least 3, and usually 4 if the structure is clear and each reason is explained.
- Use of all four sources does NOT by itself raise the score above 3. The sources must be explained and connected to advantages.
- Use of few sources does NOT cap an otherwise well-organized, well-reasoned essay at 3. It can earn a 4. But an essay with no source evidence at all and only unsupported opinion is at most 3 (usually 2 or 3).
- Off-topic claims, unsupported statistics, or illogical analogies lower the score by about one level, but do not drop an otherwise organized essay below 3.
- Surface errors alone never move an essay by more than one point.

## Calibration: 3 versus 4 versus 5
- **3 → 4:** Can you state the essay's main reasons, and does each get its own paragraph with explanation? If yes, it is a 4. If the paragraphs mostly paste or restate facts, it is a 3.
- **4 → 5:** Does the essay use several sources with specific, accurate, well-explained details, develop each paragraph fully, and close with a real conclusion? If yes, it is a 5. If the essay is clear but each paragraph is brief or one-sided in its sourcing, it is a 4.
- **5 → 6:** Only if the essay is sustained, insightful, and shows counterargument or synthesis, not just competent thoroughness.
