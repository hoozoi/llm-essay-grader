# Holistic Scoring Guide (1–6): Explanatory essay on the advantages of limiting car usage

Give ONE holistic score from 1 to 6. Judge mainly **framing (thesis/introduction/conclusion), organization around advantages, breadth and density of accurate source detail, and whether benefits are stated and explained**. Spelling and grammar matter little. Use the whole scale and do NOT default to 3. In a typical set about 7% are 1, 21% are 2, 35% are 3, 27% are 4, 9–10% are 5, and a 6 is very rare. If more than about 40% of your scores are 3, re-check: the weakest 3s are probably 2s (or 1s), and the strongest 3s are 4s. Look at your 4s too: the richest ones (3–4 sources, many specific details, framing, benefits stated) should be 5s. If you give almost no 5s, you are too strict at the top.

## Step-by-step procedure
1. **Copying/fact-list check.** Is the body mostly copied, near-copied, garbled, or a list of source facts with no thesis, no introduction or conclusion, and no real explanation (only vague throwaway reactions, such as a passing remark that something seems good)? If yes, score 1.
2. **Structure.** Is there a thesis or stated purpose about the advantages of limiting cars, ideally previewing reasons? Are body paragraphs built around advantages (pollution/health, stress, traffic, cost, safety, community life, changing culture) or only around sources? Is there a conclusion?
3. **Development.** Does the writer connect evidence (quote, statistic, example) to why it is an advantage? One-line reactions at the end of a paragraph do NOT count as development. Repeated "evidence + comment" under advantage topic sentences does, even if wordy.
4. **Sources.** Count sources used with specific, accurate details (of four), and how many details per source. Three or four sources with several specifics each is a strong sign. Thin use (one or two details per source) is weak even if all four are named. Minor factual slips and some misreadings cost little; do not drop an essay a full level just for them unless the source content is mostly garbled.
5. **Language.** Errors lower the score only when meaning is hard to follow (at most one point).

## Score levels

**6 (very rare):** A strong 5 plus a clear sense of audience, an acknowledged counterpoint answered with reasons, or genuine synthesis across sources, sustained throughout.

**5 (strong):** Thesis (often previewing advantages) in an introduction, a real conclusion, and body paragraphs that name benefits (health, cleaner air, less stress, savings, community improvement). Draws rich, mostly accurate detail (statistics, quotes, examples) from three or four sources, with several specifics per source. Paragraphs may be organized by advantage OR by source/place, as long as benefits are repeatedly stated. Explanation may be brief or repetitive, and there may be misreadings or many errors. Do NOT lower a framed, detail-rich, multi-source essay to 4 only because the explanation is "uneven" or "thin" or because one source is unused or one detail is misread. Likewise, a well-organized essay with advantage topic paragraphs, parenthetical source citations, and sustained reasoning from 3 sources is a 5.

**4 (competent):** Clear thesis, introduction, body paragraphs built around distinct reasons or advantages (or sources, if benefits are explained), a conclusion, and the writer explaining reasons in their own words. Source support may be thinner (one or two sources, fewer details), but the logic is easy to follow. Also a 4: an opinion-and-reasons essay with no sources but with intro, several distinct advantages each developed in its own paragraph, and a conclusion. Uneven explanation, errors, or few sources do not push such an essay down to 3.

**3 (developing):** A recognizable position and some relevant support, with paragraphs that have topics, but explanation of why details are advantages is only occasional. Mostly paraphrased source facts with a few real comments, generic or repetitive reasoning, loose organization, or a weak or missing introduction or conclusion. Source-by-source paragraphs with only one or two details per source and brief reactions belong at 2–3 (3 only if most paragraphs clearly state why the details are advantages).

**2 (limited):** Mainly reports what sources say, usually one paragraph per source, with only short closing remarks or brief reactions. Thesis is vague; the conclusion is generic or absent. This applies even with an intro and conclusion, three or four sources, quotes with citations, and clean writing, if the details per source are few and the commentary is one line. Also a 2: a brief or rambling opinion with a few ideas and little support, or source fragments plus a slight own point.

**1 (minimal):** No real argument or explanation.
- Copied or near-copied source sentences, or disconnected facts listed one after another, with no thesis/intro/conclusion and no explanation (even if all four sources are touched).
- Source-by-source retelling with only vague one-line reactions, especially if facts are garbled or mixed up, even with a loose opening sentence.
- Personal feelings or off-topic musings that never list or explain advantages of limiting cars, or that never use source detail; incoherent or nearly unreadable writing.
- If torn between 1 and 2 for a retelling with no own explanation, choose 1.

## Decision rules
- Mostly copied or lightly reworded source sentences, or a fact list, with no thesis and no explanation: 1. A multi-paragraph shape does not rescue it. Never 3.
- Source-by-source retelling with few details per source and one-line comments: 2 (1 if garbled, copied, or lacking thesis/intro/conclusion). Never 4, even with intro, conclusion, and three sources.
- Thesis + intro + conclusion + paragraphs organized by advantage with explained evidence: at least 4.
- Meets 4 and also uses three or more sources with multiple specifics each, benefits stated in most body paragraphs: 5.
- Using all four sources does not by itself lift an essay above 2; details must be tied to advantages.
- No source evidence: at most 4, and only if well organized by distinct advantages with real explanation. Unorganized or opinion-only essays are 1–2 (3 at most).
- Surface errors alone never move an essay more than one point. Do not penalize quotes with citations; judge the commentary around them and the density of detail.

## Calibration
- **1 vs 2:** No framing, pasted or garbled content, copied sentences, rambling, or only vague one-line reactions = 1. Own-wording report with some framing and a few genuine relevant comments = 2.
- **2 vs 3:** Each paragraph tells what one source says with a one-line reaction = 2. Paragraphs repeatedly say why the details are advantages, with a recognizable position = 3.
- **3 vs 4:** Can you list the main reasons, each with its own paragraph, own-word explanation, intro, and conclusion? Yes = 4. Mostly restated facts, occasional comments, or loose structure = 3.
- **4 vs 5:** Thesis previewing advantages, 3+ sources with many accurate specifics, benefits stated in most body paragraphs, and a real conclusion? Yes = 5. Clear but thin, one or two sources, few details per source, or noticeably list-like = 4.
- **5 vs 6:** Only with a counterpoint, synthesis, or genuine insight sustained throughout.
